Team Design Thinking & Brainstorming Canvas

Reviewer Note: This interactive board captures our team's messy, authentic brainstorming journey—from initial 5 Whys root cause analysis, raw sticky note clustering, and Crazy Eights rapid sketches, to our Idea Graveyard and final 3-tier solution architecture.

1. The "5 Whys" Root Cause Chain Root Analysis
WHY #1: Why do group vacations consistently leave friends frustrated? → Because someone always feels forced into compromises they dislike.
WHY #2: Why is someone forced into compromises? → Because the itinerary was treated as a single, rigid, all-or-nothing schedule.
WHY #3: Why was the itinerary rigid? → Because one friend (the organizer) had to manually reconcile everyone's competing wishes.
WHY #4: Why did the organizer carry the whole load? → Because planning was scattered across hundreds of chat messages and static spreadsheets.
ROOT: Current chat tools don't distinguish flexible "wants" from non-negotiable health, dietary, and budget constraints.

*What this shows: We peeled back superficial complaints ("food arguments") to identify that coordination fails because chat tools treat life-critical safety constraints as casual conversation.*

2. Problem Tree: The Anatomy of Vacation Conflict Root-to-Leaf Model

🌱 Root Causes

  • Allergies & Halal needs buried in 400+ messages
  • Stamina clash: 15k-step walkers vs slow cafe-goers
  • Schedule fragility: 1 delay cascades across all day
  • Asymmetric budgets: Students vs working peers

🪵 Core Bottleneck

The trip organizer becomes the manual integration layer between chat, spreadsheets, and maps.

🍃 Observed Symptoms

  • Organizer burnout & abandoned planning
  • Allergen panic or accidental cross-contamination
  • Compromise fatigue & passive-aggressive tension
  • Budget overruns and awkward expense splitting

*What this shows: Deep systemic constraints feed directly into the central organizer bottleneck, driving the acute symptoms that ruin group trips.*

4. "Crazy Eights" Rapid Ideation Matrix 8 Concepts in 8 Mins
1. Travel MBTI Quiz ❌ DROPPED
Gimmicky. Real constraints (allergies, budgets) are facts, not personality types.
2. Budget Bidding War ❌ DROPPED
Created toxic gamification where students felt outspent by working peers.
3. Magnetic Timeline ✅ KEPT
30-min snapping grid with visual collision alerts turns negotiation into a puzzle.
4. WhatsApp Auto-Vote Bot ❌ DROPPED
Notification spam created high cognitive load and was quickly muted.
5. Hard-Constraint Gate ✅ KEPT (MOAT)
Deterministic layer blocking hallucinated restaurants that fail dietary rules.
6. Split & Reconverge ✅ KEPT
Bifurcates groups into tailored sub-branches and converges for dinner.
7. Video Link Scraper ⚠️ PHASE 2
Auto-extract coordinates from TikTok/IG reels; deferred to focus on core logic.
8. Rain Self-Healing ⚠️ ROADMAP
Precipitation > 70% auto-triggers indoor swaps; queued for live weather API.

*What this shows: Rapid ideation filtered out flashy distractions (MBTI tests, voting bots) to isolate our three architectural pillars: the magnetic timeline, safety gate, and split routing.*

5. The Idea Graveyard (What We Deliberately Cut) Design Discipline
🪦 Dropped: Anonymous Veto Buttons

Anonymous vetoes led to social paranoia in friend circles ("Who killed my cafe idea?"). We learned that transparent split-and-merge is far healthier than covert vetoes.

🪦 Dropped: Fully Autonomous AI Bookings

Letting an agent unilaterally lock reservations produced anxiety. Travelers wanted agency: "AI drafts, deterministic code verifies, the group approves."

*What this shows: We prioritized psychological safety and user agency over black-box automation.*
